# Go-To-Market

*Scoped for a portfolio-stage launch (validating the concept with real users), not a funded commercial launch. Business-model depth intentionally stays light here — see `PRD.md` Section 10 for why.*

## Positioning

**For** online shoppers who've been burned by inconsistent cross-brand sizing,
**FitMatch is** a size-prediction tool
**that** predicts your size in a brand you've never worn, using your own fit history instead of requiring body measurements or brand data,
**unlike** existing size-recommendation tools (True Fit, Fit Analytics), which depend on brand-side integration most independent shoppers can't access.

## Target Segment for Initial Launch

Not "everyone who shops online" — too broad to test meaningfully. The narrow first audience: people who've specifically complained about cross-brand sizing friction (the same people identified during problem discovery), reached through direct outreach rather than broad marketing, since the goal right now is validated learning, not scale.

## Messaging

- **Core message:** "You already know your size — you just don't know it in *this* brand's language. FitMatch translates it."
- **Proof point to lead with:** the score breakdown showing *why* a size was predicted, not just the answer — addresses the natural skepticism of "why would I trust this."
- **What NOT to lead with:** AI/ML terminology. The audience cares about not wasting money on returns, not about the modeling approach. "AI-powered" is a footnote, not the headline.

## Launch Channels (for validation-stage testing, not paid acquisition)

- Direct outreach to the people already interviewed during problem discovery — warmest, most honest feedback available
- The LinkedIn case-study series — reaches a PM/recruiting audience, not necessarily end users, but relevant for the actual goal (portfolio, Master's applications)
- Relevant subreddits where the original complaint-hunting happened (e.g. fashion/shopping communities) — only if genuinely useful to that community, not as a growth tactic disguised as help

## Success Metrics for This Launch

Tied to `PRD.md` Section 9: prediction agreement rate across 8-10 real testers, not signups or traffic — vanity metrics would be actively misleading at this stage, since the open question is "does the core mechanism work," not "can this acquire users."

## What's Deliberately Not Here

Pricing, paid acquisition strategy, retention/growth loops, competitive positioning against incumbents at a commercial level. Building these out now, before the core prediction mechanism is validated with real users, would be planning ahead of evidence — the same mistake the wardrobe-app direction risked making.
