# Failure Modes

Where this breaks, and what limits the damage.

| Failure | Root cause | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Overconfident prediction from thin data | Confidence was originally based only on score gap, not sample size, so 1 owned item could read "high confidence" | **Fixed** — confidence drops one level when fewer than 3 owned items of the target's measurement type are logged, and the message says why |
| Heuristic doesn't generalize across body types | Ease-per-fit-rating values are hand-picked from one set of assumptions, not learned from diverse fit data | Confidence scoring exists precisely to signal uncertainty rather than force a false-authority answer |
| Single owned item: fit rating has no effect | With one item, "tight / true / loose" shifts the body estimate and the preferred ease by the same amount, so they cancel | **Documented, not fixed** — locked in by a test, so a future fix fails it on purpose as a reminder to update this page |
| Cut varies per product, not per brand | A size chart describes a regular cut. An oversized tee in XS can fit looser than a regular M, and a stretchy slim tee can measure smaller than the body wearing it. Logging one skews the body estimate and can even raise confidence. Reported in user feedback (an oversized H&M tee). | **Partly mitigated** — owned items marked oversized or slim are set aside and shown, never used. The cut of the product being bought adjusts the result by assumed amounts (+10 cm oversized, −4 cm slim); confidence is capped at medium and the page says the amounts are assumptions. Not solved: unmarked items aren't detected, and the amounts aren't measured yet (evidence so far in `open-questions.md`). |
| Size-chart data that isn't the brand's own | Some brands publish no usable body chart (Zara), or their source can't be checked (Zudio) | **Labelled, not hidden** — three flags, each with its own visible note on any result it touches: *approximate* (Zara, converted from a garment chart; also caps confidence at medium), *unverified* (women's Zudio, third-party site), *placeholder* (men's Zudio, averaged from real men's charts). Tests check each flag matches its label in the data. |
| Brand's chart differs by product line | Uniqlo, Max and Nike/Jordan each publish different body charts for different lines | One line is stored per brand (named in its `_source`); the others are logged in `open-questions.md`. A user buying from a different line may be a size off. |
| Charts with fewer measurements look better than they are | A chest-only chart summed fewer weighted terms than a chest/waist/hip chart, so it scored lower (better) for no real reason | **Fixed in v8** — scores are a weighted average over the measurements actually compared |
| Cross-measurement-type prediction | Garment and body measurements are different physical quantities; mixing them once crashed the engine and could otherwise give a silent wrong answer | **Fixed** — estimates are kept separate per type, and a cross-type request returns "No fit history yet" instead of a guess. Since v8 every chart is body type, so this is a safety net; it stays tested with a synthetic garment chart. |

**Design principle:** the system should always surface something honest — a low-confidence flag, a labelled data warning — rather than a clean-looking answer that's silently wrong.
